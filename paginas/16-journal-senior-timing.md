**Byline:** By Lisa Weiss · It's A Keeper Photography · Richland, WA

**H1:** When Should You Take Senior Pictures?

**Hero intro:** Summer before senior year and early fall are the most common
windows, but the right time is the one that fits your yearbook deadline, your
schedule and your style.

**Hero script:** your season

**Hero button:** Read the timeline → `#the-short-answer` (botón local, no anchor)

**H2: The Short Answer**

Most seniors take their pictures the **summer before senior year or in early
fall**, ahead of yearbook deadlines. But after twenty years of photographing
seniors in the Tri-Cities, my honest answer is: the best time is the one that
matches *your* season — and it's almost never actually "too late."

**H2: When Are You Supposed to Take Senior Pictures?**

There's no official rule — just a practical one: your school's yearbook
deadline. Check the deadline published by your school, then work backward so
you have time to photograph, choose your favorites, and submit without stress.
A summer or early-fall session often gives you that breathing room; see how
senior sessions work.

Beyond the yearbook, families time sessions around sports seasons, hair
appointments, braces coming off, and — smart ones — the weather they love most.

**H2: Season by Season: What Each One Gives You**

**H3: Spring of junior year**

Blossoms, green fields and soft light — and you beat the senior-year rush
entirely. Choosing spring means your photos are done before applications,
sports and lasts-of-everything take over.

**H3: Summer before senior year**

The most popular window, for good reason: long golden evenings, tan lines
optional, fewer school-schedule conflicts. In the Tri-Cities, summer golden
hour is generous — warm light that lasts and lasts. Book early; these dates go
first.

**H3: Early fall of senior year**

October along the river is the Tri-Cities showing off — autumn color, honey
light, sweater weather. It's also yearbook-deadline season, so fall sessions
book out fastest of all.

**H3: Winter**

Underrated. Clean light, quiet locations, moody skies — and a calendar with far
more open dates. If your style leans dramatic or cozy, winter might be your
season.

**H2: When Is It Too Late to Take Senior Pictures?**

Here's the answer most websites won't give you: **it's only too late for the
yearbook — never for the photographs.** If the yearbook deadline has passed,
submit whatever photo the school needs and then take your real session whenever
it works. Senior pictures aren't for the yearbook page; they're for you, your
parents, your graduation announcements — and the version of you that only
existed this year. I've photographed wonderful senior sessions in February, in
April, even after graduation. Those photos matter just as much.

**H2: What's the Best Time of Day for Senior Pictures Outside?**

Golden hour — roughly the last 60 to 90 minutes before sunset. The light turns
warm and directional, skin looks its best, and backgrounds glow instead of
glare. In the Tri-Cities that means an evening session almost year-round; exact
timing shifts with the season, and I plan it to the minute for every shoot.

**H2: The Tri-Cities Booking Calendar (What Nobody Tells You)**

Senior dates fill faster than anything else I photograph. My practical advice,
learned from twenty years of local booking patterns: reserve your session
**four to six weeks before** the date you want, and earlier for the golden
windows — summer evenings and October. If you're reading this in spring wanting
a fall session, now is exactly the right time to reach out.

**H2: Quick Answers**

**H3: Junior or senior year?**

Either. Spring junior year or summer/fall senior year are both ideal.

**H3: Before or after getting braces off?**

After, if the timing is close — or we celebrate the smile you have now. Your
call.

**H3: Is winter too cold?**

We plan around it: shorter sets, warm layers between shots, and light that's
worth it.

**H2: Whenever You Shoot, Make It Yours**

The timeline matters less than this: your senior pictures should look like
*you* — your style, your places, your story. That's what we plan together, and
it's the part I've loved for twenty years.
