**H2: What We'll Photograph**

A branding gallery usually covers four things:

**You** — portraits that feel like your brand rather than like a passport photo. These double as your headshots.

**Your work** — hands, tools, process, craft. The evidence that you actually do the thing.

**Your space** — the studio, shop, office or corner of the world where it happens.

**Your details** — products, textures, materials, the small things your clients notice and you've stopped seeing.

**H2: About Usage Rights — Read This Part**

This is where most photography gets confusing, so let me be plain.

When you book a **family session**, your images come with personal print rights: print them for your home, share them on social media, send them to grandma.

When you book a **branding session**, you're getting something different — a commercial usage license. Those images are yours to use to promote your business: your website, ads, a business card, a billboard if you're feeling ambitious. That's why branding is priced differently from the start. You're not paying for more photographs; you're paying for broader rights to use them.

I'd rather explain this clearly up front than have you find out later that the photograph you loved wasn't licensed for what you needed. Book it as branding and it's covered.

**H2: Branding Questions**

**H3: What's the difference between branding photos and headshots?**
A headshot is one great portrait of you. Branding photography is a full library — you, your work, your space, your details. Every branding session includes headshots, so if you're unsure, branding covers both.

**H3: Can I use these images for advertising?**
Yes. Branding sessions include commercial usage rights, which is exactly what separates them from a personal session.

**H3: Where do branding sessions happen?**
Usually at your place of business anywhere in Richland, Kennewick or Pasco. If you don't have a physical space, we'll choose Tri-Cities locations that match your brand.

**H3: Can my team be included?**
Yes — team portraits and candid working shots are a natural part of many branding sessions.

**H3: How often should branding photos be updated?**
Most businesses refresh once or twice a year as offers, spaces and seasons change. Headshots hold up longer — every two to three years, or after any big change.

**H3: What should I wear?**
Your brand's colors, your real working clothes, and one more polished option. We plan it together — the goal is recognizably you, one notch elevated.
